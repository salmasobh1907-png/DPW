Logistics margins are thin and Sokhna now has a rival terminal: Egypt needs a fast, rule-based pricing layer

DP World is investing heavily in Egyptian logistics, but forwarding earns a fraction of port margins and competition is rising. Every quote we lose, or win too cheaply, matters. The prototype shows how one internal web app can make quoting faster and protect margin, while feeding from the Rate Bank today and NPM / CARGOES later instead of replacing them.

The ask

  1. Approve a pilot of the tool as one internal web app hosted by IT (Entra ID sign-in, Pricing vs Sales roles).
  2. Hold the BTI data-sources meeting: Rate Bank on SharePoint (read only), shared mailboxes via Microsoft Graph (read only), then NPM.
  3. Name one data owner each for rates, FX and customs (ACI) so the rules have a trusted source.

What the public record says

Strengths

  • Over USD 1.4bn invested in Egyptian logistics: Sokhna port, Sokhna Logistics Park in SCZONE, a Logistics Distribution Centre opened July 2026, five offices. [1][2]
  • Group logistics revenue grew about 28% in 2025. [3]
  • CARGOES Runner already exists for forwarder quoting and booking. [9]

Risks

  • Logistics EBITDA margin about 14% vs about 49% in ports: small leaks in margin matter. [3]
  • Red Sea Container Terminal at Sokhna opened January 2026; some carriers moved calls to it. [4]
  • Fragmented market with DHL, DSV, Kuehne+Nagel, CEVA. [5]
  • EGP above 56 per USD; demurrage and storage about USD 150 per box per day. [6]
  • ACI is mandatory for air cargo too since 1 January 2026. [7]

Opportunities

  • Non-oil exports up about 17% in 2025. [8]
  • Suez Canal traffic recovering: routings and rates will keep moving, so speed of re-quoting wins. [10]
  • Bundles of port, park, warehouse and forwarding (our inference).
