Fully automatic version (needs DP World IT)

  1. Monthly request sends itself: a Power Automate flow on the 25th sends “Rate Idea for <next month> - DP World” to the carrier list.
  2. Replies file themselves: a second flow catches replies with “Rate Idea for” in the subject and saves the Excel files and email text in SharePoint › Carrier Offers › <month> › <carrier>.
  3. Offers read straight into this page: once IT approves the Microsoft 365 connector, saved offers open here without dropping files.
  4. Alerts in Teams or Outlook: a daily 8:00 flow posts rates that end within 5 days and new sales feedback.

Until then, Carrier offers turns any offer into rows in seconds, and this page prepares every request email in one click each.

Two views in one platform

  • Pricing view (pricing team): Inbox, Rates & offer (send the offer to sales), Offers & feedback, the Rate Bank (with carrier offers, Ask carriers, Pricing log and Rate check), and Reports.
  • Sales view (sales team): Inbox, Find a rate, and My offers & feedback: pricing's offers, the quote to the customer, hit ratio, and feedback to pricing with the answer.
  • The page owner and anyone added under Offers & feedback › Pricing team can switch between the two views at the top.

Where the data comes from

  1. Rates: Rate Bank Export.xlsx on SharePoint, sheet Rate Data Base. A rate with no validity date counts as valid to the end of its month. “Lowest” uses standard dry rates only (spot, special, named-account, reefer and DG rows are left out). Import rates: Rate Bank Import.xlsx.
  2. Customer requests: the Reference Sheet in Rate Bank Export.xlsx, one line per request (quotation number, customer, ports, containers, status, sales and pricing person). Won = status WON (older rows: ACC).
  3. Alliance loops: carrier and trade-press notices collected by hand on 19 Oct 2026, 12 rotations that call Egypt. Reference only, not live.
  4. Cross-border trucking: demo trucker offer (dummy), plus USD 100 per truck.
  5. Coastlines: Natural Earth world outlines, built into this file.

What every label means

  • Sea time: distance in km ÷ about 710 km a day. Only the hours the ship is moving, so it is shorter than what a line quotes.
  • Line transit (TT): transit time carriers gave us in the Rate Bank. Typical = middle value. Includes port calls and transhipment. Use this one with customers.
  • ~ days: no transit time in the bank, so sea time + about 3 days in port.
  • Cargo and container: Dry shows standard 20' and 40' rates. Reefer shows the rows with Contract Type “Reefer” plus any 20RF / 40RF on other rows. Reefer never counts in “lowest”.
  • THC, free time, carrier note: origin THC 20' / 40' and free time as the carrier gave them; the note icon shows the Rate Bank comment (hover to read it all).
  • Offer to the customer: the options you tick, with sell = buy + margin (+ THC if you choose “Include”). The customer email shows sell rates only.
  • Margin: USD 100 per container as standard. Dry export to China: USD 25 per 20' and USD 50 per 40'. Pricing can type another margin for one lane; sales always get the rule.
  • Valid until: the quotation takes the carrier rate's own validity from the Rate Bank. With no valid carrier rate it is 14 days from today.
  • Carrier volumes: the Bookings sheet in Rate Bank Export, per carrier: shipments, containers, TEU and share.
  • Hit ratio: requests won ÷ all requests in that month. Example: 3 won out of 12 = 25%.
  • Rates ending soon: in the bell at the top, rates whose validity ends within 5 days, one line per carrier with a ready extension email.
  • Door time (alternatives): sea time + port time carriers add on the lane + truck time (road km ÷ 550 + 1 day per border + ½ day handling).

How the route is worked out

  • Ships follow a network of about 170 sea waypoints (straits, canals, capes). The shortest path through it is the route.
  • A closure removes that passage and the route is recalculated; if nothing is left the lane shows “No sea route”.
  • All figures are a snapshot of the Rate Bank taken on . The page refreshes hourly on working days when the Rate Bank changes.
